# Cast from Windows

To see what's on your computer on the screen, with its sound. You need a computer with Windows 10 or 11 and Wi-Fi, on the same network as the screen.

## Before you start

On the screen, open the **Cast** app and check:

- That **Cast** is turned on. At the top it says “Ready to receive as” followed by your screen's name.
- That **Windows and Android** is on under **Sources**.
- That **Sound only** is off. With it on, the screen isn't offered for mirroring.

## Connect

1. On the computer, press the **Windows** key and **K** at the same time. The list of displays opens on the right.
2. Choose your screen's name, for example **Living room**.
3. The first time, the screen asks “Let … send to this screen?”. Tap **Allow**. After that, that computer connects by itself.

You can now see the computer on the screen, with its sound.

> [!TIP]
> The computer needs Wi-Fi even if it's connected by cable: Windows looks for displays over Wi-Fi.

If you chose **Anyone at home** in Cast, the screen doesn't ask anything: it connects straight away.

## Stop casting

- On the computer: press **Windows** + **K** again and click **Disconnect**.
- On the screen: swipe down from the top to see the notifications and, in the “Casting from” notification, tap **Stop receiving**.

## Single photos and videos

To show a photo or a video without mirroring the whole computer, right-click the file, choose **Cast to Device** and then your screen. In Windows 11 it's under **Show more options**.

For this, **Photos, videos and music** has to be on under **Sources**. There's more on this in [Photos, videos and music](fotos-videos-y-musica.md).

## If the screen doesn't show up

- Check that the computer and the screen are on the same network. Guest networks on routers usually don't let devices see each other.
- Go over [Before you start](#before-you-start) again.
- If Windows says the computer can't project, it's the computer's Wi-Fi that doesn't support it. Single photos and videos still work.

> [!WARNING]
> Paid movies and series show up black: their copy protection doesn't allow sending them to another screen.
