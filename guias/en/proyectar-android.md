# Cast from Android

To see what's on your Android phone or tablet on the screen. Many phones have their own button for casting; for those that don't, there's the “Send to Home” app. Either way, the phone has to be on the same Wi-Fi as the screen.

## Before you start

On the screen, open the **Cast** app and check that it's turned on and that **Windows and Android** is on under **Sources**. **Sound only** has to be off.

## With your phone's button

1. Swipe down from the top to open Quick Settings. You may have to swipe twice.
2. Tap the cast or screen-sharing button. Each brand names it differently.
3. Choose your screen, for example **Living room**.
4. If the phone asks for a PIN, enter the one shown on the screen.
5. The first time, the screen asks “Let … send to this screen?”. Tap **Allow**.

To stop, tap the same button on the phone again.

## With the “Send to Home” app

For phones with Android 10 or later that don't have a cast button.

1. Install “Send to Home” on the phone. You can download it from the [Home releases](https://github.com/daniel1286/home-rom-rg3205w/releases/latest).
2. Open it. It finds the Home screens on your Wi-Fi by itself: choose yours.
3. If you also want to hear the sound from apps, turn on **Send sound too**. Android asks for the microphone permission for this, but the microphone isn't used.
4. Accept when Android asks whether to allow recording the screen.
5. The first time, accept on the screen to let the phone send.

To stop, tap **Stop sending** in the phone's notification.

## If the screen doesn't show up

- Check that the phone and the screen are on the same network. Guest networks on routers usually don't let devices see each other.
- Go over [Before you start](#before-you-start) again.
- If it doesn't show up with the phone's button, try “Send to Home”.

> [!WARNING]
> Paid movies and series show up black: their copy protection doesn't allow sending them to another screen.
